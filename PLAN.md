# PLAN

How the milestones of SPEC §13 get built. SPEC.md says what; FORMAT.md says the bytes; this
file only orders the work. Status: approved 2026-11-08; milestones 2, 3, 4 and 5 closed (3 on
2026-11-10, 4 on 2026-11-11, 5 on 2026-11-13); milestone 6: built, verify pending.

## Milestone 2: toy and synthetic

**Goal:** the whole pipeline, `blobc build` → `blobc verify --tier tiny`, runs on synthetic
inputs. The inputs are real `.osm.pbf` files and Overture parquet, generated by the
`fixtures` module, so nothing in the pipeline is test-only.

### Modules, in build order

Two crates (SPEC §10): `blobmap-model` holds `format` and `runtime`; `blobc` holds every
other module and builds the one `blobc` binary. Each module gets unit tests as it lands. "Old" is `~/Work/blobmap/blobmap-compiler`;
"research" is `~/Work/blobmap/the-new-blobmap-model`.

1. **`format`**: the FORMAT.md writer and reader.
   - **Ports:** old `format/io.rs`, the deterministic safetensors writer and validating
     reader.
   - **Rewrites:** old `model.rs`: manifest and index types, hashes (FORMAT §9),
     validation (§10).
   - **New:** tensors ordered by element size so they are aligned; chunking along
     dimension 0; filling shards unit by unit; `inputs_hash`; ignoring unknown manifest
     keys.
2. **`fixtures`**: synthetic inputs (all new).
   - A minimal `.osm.pbf` writer: protobuf and zlib by hand, checked by reading the files
     back with `osmpbf`.
   - A writer for Overture `address`/`place` parquet and taxonomy CSVs.
   - **Scenarios:** the toy town; islands; one-way traps; a footbridge that joins two parts
     for walkers only; routes just over and just under the cap; equal-time ties; via-way
     `no_*`/`only_*` with one and two via ways; same-piece pairs (one-way and two-way,
     equal offsets); aliases and filtered places; a graph that a small budget forces into
     cells.
   - **Defined early** so every later module tests on real files.
3. **`profile`**: profiles and place rules as versioned TOML, with their hashes.
   - **Ports:** old `profile` (the OSRM v6 car rules, the ACTIVE values of OSRM's
     bicycle/foot).
   - **New:** the place-rules file, and the eligibility rules in the profile (SPEC §6).
4. **`osm`**: reading the `.pbf`.
   - **Ports:** old `osm` (ways, pieces split at intersections, lengths, obstacles and
     their directions), and the restriction parsing of old `edge/edge.rs`.
   - **New:** keeping via-way restrictions (one or more via ways) instead of skipping
     them.
5. **`graph`**: the mode-family graphs and their components.
   - **Ports:** old `graph` and `edge` (CAR turn graph, OSRM turn costs, u-turns,
     node-via restrictions), the ACTIVE union graph, SCC.
   - **New:**
     - **Via-way copies:** old `edge/restrict.rs` (Aho–Corasick states) is ported, and
       gets `only_*` rewriting, two or more via ways, and copy rows (`kind = 2`,
       `copy_of`, `copies`).
     - **Per-way rounding** (SPEC §7) on the stored `f32` times.
     - **Trapped detection:** see the reading under "Open items".
     - **Component numbering** (FORMAT §4).
6. **`order`**: the node order and cells.
   - **Ports:** RoutingKit inertial-flow nested dissection, vendored with its OpenMP
     patch, whose order is bit-identical across thread counts (old `order`).
   - **New:**
     - an `Orderer` trait, with nested dissection and a fixed order for the toy town;
     - cells: order the fine graph as in one piece, cut the elimination tree of that
       order where subtrees fit `memory_budget_bytes`, keep the order in every block
       (SPEC §5). Fallback when the fine graph is too large to order: coarsen, take the top
       separators from the coarse ordering, lift them to the fine graph so that no fine arc
       joins two cells; each cell and the top block are then ordered on their own.
7. **`cch`**: the hierarchy.
   - **Ports:** old `contract` (chordal completion, elimination tree, levels),
     `customize`, `layout`.
   - **New:**
     - blocks (a top block then cells, level order inside a block);
     - packs and link references across packs (FORMAT §4);
     - customizing blocks in order (cells before the top);
     - deriving triangles;
     - the 2³² pack limits.
8. **`overture`**: the Overture inputs.
   - **Ports:** old `overture` (parquet reading, taxonomy, redirects, confidence and closed
     filters, the 30 m dedup) and old `geo/region.rs` (`.poly` containment) for
     `--extent`.
   - **New:** aliases for merged places; `FilteredPlace`; `NONE16` for keys missing from
     the taxonomy; the UUID check that fails the build; the two extent modes (SPEC §3).
9. **`snap`**: IDs onto roads.
   - **Ports:** old `snap` (local tangent plane, grid index, nearest piece, foot of the
     perpendicular, at-node rule).
   - **New:** snapping per mode for bike and walk; skipping trapped pieces and copies;
     offsets by the rounding rule (SPEC §7); the four reason codes.
10. **`build`**: the stages behind a `Stage` trait.
    - **Rewrites:** old `pipeline`.
    - **New:**
      - packs shared by small components;
      - the model writer (packs, shards, manifest, `LICENSES`);
      - the data report (per-country and per-category counts, trapped components, `NONE16`
        places);
      - the build report (wall time and peak RSS per stage, largest pack per family);
      - both reports written outside the model directory.
11. **`runtime`**: the CPU reference runtime.
    - **Ports:** old `refruntime` (sweeps, multi-source columns, bounded sweeps, seeds,
      `EdgeDijkstra`, re-customization).
    - **New:**
      - car `to` heads through the exit turns;
      - arrivals through copies;
      - the direct term;
      - aliases and reasons;
      - every operation of SPEC §9, `multi_criteria` included.
12. **`verify`**: the conformance suite: the tiers, the checks of SPEC §11, golden files (FORMAT §11), and
    the mutants.
    - **Ports:** old `conformance`.
    - **New:** the turn-aware Dijkstra that tracks the segments driven, independent of the
      copy construction; the via-way, cells, cap, ties and car-`to` cases.
    - **Memory:** the checks on the model's bytes run first, with no reference; above the
      tiny tier the reference is then built and checked one family at a time; each check
      holds one of the model's components at a time; the golden replay holds no reference.
13. **`cli`** (`blobc`'s `main.rs`): `blobc build | verify | inspect`, thin over `build`,
    `verify` and `blobmap-model`, and the tools (`fixtures`, `extract`, `tier`,
    `accuracy`, `components`, `fragments`) as subcommands with their own arguments.

### Gate check

The gate passes on a clean commit when all of these hold:

1. **Unit tests:** `cargo test --workspace` passes, with zero warnings under
   `cargo clippy`.
2. **Tiny tier:** for every fixture scenario, `blobc build` then `blobc verify --tier
   tiny` shows zero mismatches on every check of SPEC §11 that applies, and golden files
   are written and replayed. Specifically:
   - the toy town gives its 17 links, 5 levels and distance grid;
   - every mutant fails.
3. **Determinism:** `tools/determinism.sh` builds every scenario with 1, 2, 8 and 24
   threads (`RAYON_NUM_THREADS` and `OMP_NUM_THREADS`). The SHA-256 of every file in the
   model directory must be equal across runs.
4. **Cells:** the cells scenario built with a small budget and in one piece gives
   different `model_id`s but the same `inputs_hash`, and replays the same golden file.

`ci.sh` runs 1–4 locally (SPEC §11: every commit).

Decided at approval: trapped components follow SPEC §5 ("Precisely"), and the data report
compares against `--previous-report` (SPEC §3, §10).

## Optimisation, after milestone 6

1. **The serial pack writer.** The hierarchy stages ran 6.9× (car) and 8.4× (active) their
   time estimates on the planet: each cell's pack is assembled on one thread from random
   reads into mapped work columns that no longer fit the page cache, so the build waits on
   one page fault at a time. Assemble the packs of a batch in parallel and write them in
   order.

## Runtime: `blobmap-runtime`, the GPU serving library

**Goal:** a library crate, `crates/blobmap-runtime`, that loads a model onto a GPU and
answers every operation of SPEC §9 exactly as `blobmap_model::reference` does, as fast as
the hardware allows. The BlobMap API imports it; there is no server and no binary beyond
test and benchmark tools. SPEC.md and FORMAT.md stay the authorities: the runtime reads the
bytes as they are and changes neither document.

### Repo rules

- **Dependencies:** `blobmap-runtime` depends on `blobmap-model` only, never on `blobc`.
  `tools/runtime-deps.sh`, run by `ci.sh`, fails when `cargo tree -p blobmap-runtime`
  names `blobc`.
- **No CUDA needed to build:** the crate is a workspace member left out of
  `default-members`. Its GPU code is CubeCL 0.11.4 (Rust kernels), on CubeCL's CUDA runtime
  and its wgpu runtime. The CUDA driver and NVRTC are loaded at run time, so no CUDA toolkit
  or nvcc is needed: `cargo build`, `cargo test --workspace`, clippy and `ci.sh` work on a
  machine without CUDA. There is no `cuda` feature and no `.cu` file.
- **Backends:** three, all first class: CPU, CubeCL cuda and CubeCL wgpu (here through
  Vulkan). Each passes every golden at zero mismatches at each milestone gate and has its
  own timings. Metal and HIP are untested until they pass the full gate on their own
  hardware.
- **Branch:** all work happens on the branch `runtime`, in the main checkout (no
  worktree), and merges to `main` only after a gate passes.
  `crates/blobc` is not touched, apart from the one mechanical rename below.
- **GPU use:**
  - free VRAM is checked before every run, and each run stays inside its budget;
  - no other process is ever stopped;
  - heavy jobs run one at a time, under a memory-capped systemd scope;
  - the live BlobMap API is never queried.

### Step 0: the reference runtime renamed

- `blobmap_model::runtime` becomes `blobmap_model::reference`, so that "runtime" always
  means this crate.
- The compiler's `use` paths change with it: 17 lines in `crates/blobc/src/verify/*` and
  `main.rs`, imports only.
- **Proof:** `ci.sh` green, and HdF rebuilt and verified with the same model_id as before.

### Design

**Loading** (per family, VRAM budget given at load):
- open the model through `blobmap-model`, which memory-maps the shards;
- **GPU, hot:** `level_first`, `up_first`, `up_head`, the metric `up`/`down` tensors,
  `nodes/component` and the block tables;
- **host RAM, warm:** turns, roads, segment weights, copies and the vocabulary;
- **cold tensors are never read:** way keys and master times.
- The CPU upward search reads the hot tensors through the same mapping, so they also sit in
  the page cache.
- Load fails with a clear error when the hot set, the entry bounds and the label pool for
  B_max columns exceed the budget. Inside it, the label pool sets how many columns can be
  in flight.
- **As built at R1:** a component is loaded on first use. Its graph is copied to the host
  and relayered top-first by longest-path level (new ids, rows sorted by head), instead of
  using the format's own levels. It then goes to the device under an LRU VRAM budget.
  The vocabulary tables are borrowed from the mapping, with no host copy.

**Seeds and upward search on the CPU.**
- Sources resolve to their slots and offsets, plus the car exit turns for `from`, or the
  arrivals through copies for `to` (FORMAT §8).
- The upward search climbs the thin chain of ancestors on the host. Its labels go to the
  GPU as one sparse seed list per column.

**Downward sweep on the GPU**, ported from the research's `cuda/sweep.cu` to CubeCL
kernels (u32 words only, u16 labels packed two per word):
- **Top block:** resident in shared memory, as `top_resident`; it fits on France (20 KB).
- **Wide levels:** one plain launch per wide level, chunked, with lane groups for
  high-degree nodes. CUDA graph capture was tried and removed: it gave wrong
  `chained_column` labels and capture errors (India, Japan, FR+5) at the same speed as
  plain launches, cause unknown.
- **Cells:** below the top block, cells share no links (FORMAT §5.2). Each cell's levels
  run as independent work, one thread block per cell for its narrow and medium levels,
  instead of one launch per cell level. This attacks the 972 launches that cost France
  7.4 ms against the research's 5.7.
- **Batching:** B columns per sweep, B = 1 to 64, with labels interleaved per node. A
  dispatcher merges concurrent requests on the same (family, metric, component, dir) into
  one sweep.

**Pruning** (isochrone, nearest, reachable and bounded multi-criteria terms):
- per elimination-tree subtree, with the entry bound of the study (§5.5): an upper
  neighbour's label ⊕ the cheapest link into the subtree;
- the entry bound is 2 B per link per metric, computed at load on the CPU by one bottom-up
  merge (51 s for the Europe car component) and stored on the GPU;
- two variants, measured on France and Europe with the faster one kept:
  - (a) a per-node live flag passed down level by level;
  - (b) a depth-first renumbering at load, so that every subtree is a range skipped in one
    jump.
- A full `column` is never pruned.

**Heads, exactly as FORMAT §8:**
- offsets;
- car exit turns, and arrivals through copies;
- the direct term on a shared piece;
- same ID, the cap, `BEYOND`, `DifferentComponent` from `nodes/component`;
- the smallest-ID tie-break (SPEC §7).
- **Pruned queries:** only the visited labels come back and the heads run on the host.
- **Full columns:** two options, both measured on Europe at B = 1 and B = 64, keeping the
  faster:
  - copy the labels back (Europe car 288 MB per column) and run the heads in parallel on
    the host;
  - upload the family's per-ID entry tables, a warm tensor, and run the heads on the GPU.
  - Moving a warm tensor to the GPU is a change from the brief, so I'll make it only if the
    measurements show it pays.
- **As built at R1:** labels are read back and unpacked into columns, and the heads run on
  the host over every ID of the component. That is 92% of a Europe car column (34.5 s of
  37.3 s), so R2 moves the heads onto the GPU for cuda and wgpu, as one streaming pass on
  the CPU backend, over only the IDs the query needs.

**API:**
- **Types:**
  - `Runtime::open(model_dir, Options { vram_budget, devices, b_max, … })`;
  - typed methods for every SPEC §9 operation: `travel_time`, `isochrone`, `nearest`,
    `column`, `reachable_from_all`/`_any`, `multi_criteria`, `chained`, `chained_column`;
  - results and reasons use `blobmap_model::reference`'s own types (`Reason`, `Dir`, IDs),
    re-exported.
- **Concurrency:** `Runtime` is `Send + Sync`, queries run concurrently, each request
  thread does its own upward search, and one GPU worker per device drains the batch queue.
- **Golden replay:** a `golden` module answers `queries.jsonl` lines through the typed API,
  for replay and benchmarks.
- **CPU backend:** the same front end with a CPU downward sweep. It is a first-class
  backend, used when no GPU is present, and the second opinion in the randomized tests.

**Kernels:** CubeCL kernels in `crates/blobmap-runtime/src/gpu.rs`, compiled at run time
by each runtime (NVRTC for cuda, the wgpu shader compiler for wgpu). There is no
`build.rs`.

### Order of work

1. Step 0: rename and proof.
2. Crate skeleton:
   - dependency check, CPU backend;
   - typed operations matching the reference on the tiny tier.
3. GPU loading:
   - hot upload, VRAM budget, label pool;
   - downward sweep at B = 1 (research kernels, top resident), labels checked against the
     CPU sweep;
   - then full columns.
4. Heads, seeds and operations on the GPU path; golden replay on tiny, small and HdF.
5. Pruning, both variants: exactness against full columns, then France and Europe
   measured.
6. Per-cell levels and batching (B = 1, 8, 32, 64); the dispatcher.
7. **R1**, then R2 measurements, then the README, examples and the world extrapolation for
   R3.

### Gates

- **R1, correct** (stop and report):
  - zero mismatches on every backend (CPU, cuda, wgpu), replaying the golden files of:
    - tiny (all scenarios);
    - small: Lille, Great Britain, Japan, Fiji, Norway, India, California;
    - HdF, France and FR+5 (`frbeluchde`);
    - Europe car, active and mixed. Car fits a 16 GB card with entry bounds off and at
      most 2 columns per sweep; mixed runs with the GPU on one family at a time.
  - The development gate after each change is tiny, small and HdF on all three backends.
  - The existing models in `data/models` match the golden `inputs_hash` of their tier, so
    no rebuild is needed.
  - **Randomized comparison** with `blobmap_model::reference` on HdF and France, fixed
    seed: columns, isochrones, nearest, multi-criteria and chained, in both directions,
    with start times, same-piece pairs and border sources.
- **R2, fast** (stop and report): a query's time is dominated by the sweep, on every
  backend, with zero mismatches. Order: heads, unpack removed, per-column upload, host
  memory borrowed from the mapping (the format's own levels against the relayering),
  then the sweep (cuda at 7.44 ms or below without graphs, then the wgpu gap). Each step
  is measured before and after with the phase timers.
  - **Latency** (p50, p99, end to end) per backend and query type, with the phase
    breakdown, on France and Europe:
    - travel_time;
    - isochrone at 5/15/30/60 min, with the border sources Strasbourg, Saint-Louis and
      Menton included;
    - nearest;
    - full column;
    - multi-criteria.
  - Throughput at B = 1, 8, 32 and 64.
  - Host RAM and VRAM, steady and peak, for Europe car, active and mixed.
  - The full gate's wall time.
  - France car compared with the research's 5.7 ms and the smoke test's 7.4 ms.
- **R3, ready for the API:**
  - the public API documented in `crates/blobmap-runtime/README.md`, with examples;
  - an extrapolation to the world model on one H200 (hot 83.4 GB on the GPU, warm in RAM),
    from the R2 fits and the planet's measured sizes.
